# Citation check for the 27 obligations

Date checked: 2026-10-23. Checked by an automated research pass, not by counsel.

These are a synthetic bank's own obligations. Each one "maps to" a public rule or framework. Nothing in this file claims that a control satisfies a regulation, and the bench does not assess compliance.

How to read this file:

- Every paraphrase is in my own words and capped at 25 words per citation. The document quotes one public source once (CHG-7, 9 words). Quotation marks elsewhere refer to the design table's own wording.
- Fit labels: **direct** (the source states substantially what the obligation says), **maps to** (the source covers the topic; the bank's wording is narrower, broader or more concrete), **stretch: bank policy goes beyond the rule** (the source supports the idea only loosely; the specifics are the bank's).
- Source keys (N1, P1, ...) are defined below with full URLs. Several sources could not be read on their canonical page; the "How read" column says what was actually read. Read the section "Read through secondary sources" before relying on a row.
- Part 500 section numbers are for the text as amended 2023-11-01. As of the check date the phase-in periods in 500.22 have run, including 2025-11-01 for the 500.12 and 500.13(a) additions.

## Corrections to the original obligation table

Items 1 to 6 answer questions raised while the catalog was being written. The rest are other fixes found while checking.

1. **MFA-1, MFA scope.** MFA is 500.12, and the citation should be 500.12(a). As amended, MFA applies to any individual accessing any information system, unless a limited exemption under 500.19(a) applies, in which case it covers remote access, remote access to third-party applications holding NPI, and privileged accounts other than service accounts that prohibit interactive login (500.12(a)(1)-(3)). The all-systems scope took effect 2025-11-01. The old rule covered only access to internal networks from outside. The bank's "privileged routes" obligation is narrower than 500.12(a) for a non-exempt entity. Sources: [N1], [N2], [N3].
2. **INV-1, asset inventory.** 500.13(a) is correct. Add (a)(1)(i) owner and (a)(1)(iii) classification or sensitivity. FFIEC AIO III.B.1 is a useful second cite.
3. **AUD-2, retention.** The retention periods are in 500.6(b), but "5 (rule)" is incomplete. There are two floors: five years for the 500.6(a)(1) transaction-reconstruction records and three years for the 500.6(a)(2) cybersecurity audit trails. Append-only and hash-chaining are not in the rule. Cite 500.6(a)(1)-(2) for AUD-1 as well.
4. **EXC-1, CISO compensating controls.** Part 500 lets the CISO approve compensating controls in four places: 500.12(b) (MFA), 500.15(b) (at-rest encryption infeasible), and, for Class A companies only, 500.7(c)(2) (common-password blocking) and 500.14(b) (endpoint detection and centralized logging). Not in 500.4. All require written approval and annual review. The approver is the CISO, not a "risk owner". The register, expiry and component scoping are bank design. Replace the vague "CC3/CC5" with CC5.1 (alternative controls when segregation is impractical), CC4.2 (deficiency tracking) and CC5.3.
5. **TSC change management.** CC8.1 is the change-management criterion. Confirmed. CC6.1, CC6.3, CC6.7 and C1.1 also match their uses in the table.
6. **FFIEC change management and emergency changes.** The fuller treatment is in the Development, Acquisition, and Maintenance booklet (August 2024): VII.B Change Management, VII.B.1 Implementing Changes, VII.B.3(c) Emergency Modifications, VII.B.4 documentation. The AIO booklet (June 2021) also covers it at III.D.1, which is why the original table's AIO cite for CHG-1 and CHG-7 is not wrong, but it is incomplete. CHG-3 and LAP-1 cite a bare "FFIEC" and CHG-4 cites "FFIEC D&A testing"; use DA&M VII.B.1 (CHG-3, LAP-1) and V.B (CHG-4), and VII.B.2 where segregation of duties is the point (CHG-2, CHG-6). The 2024 booklet replaced the 2004 Development and Acquisition booklet, and the AIO text still refers to the retired title.
7. **AS 2201 and ITGC.** AS 2201 and AS 2110 contain no occurrences of "ITGC" or "ITAC"; those are practitioner shorthand. Where AS 2201 discusses IT general controls: .36 (points to AS 2110.29 and Appendix B), .47 (reliance on general controls as a risk factor), .60 and Appendix B B28-B33 (benchmarking automated controls depends on effective general controls over program changes, access to programs and computer operations). The detail on IT risks, including segregation of duties, is in AS 2110 Appendix B (B1, B4). Replace "SOX ITGC access", "SOX ITGC program change" and "SOX ITGC SoD" with these paragraphs. The design intro should name AS 2110 as well as AS 2201.
8. **MC-1, PAY-1, LIM-1, CRD-1, PAY-2.** These cite "SOX ITAC" or "SOX application control" with no section. The PCAOB text offers at most AS 2201.B28-B33 on automated application controls. Preparer and approver separation, dual approval and thresholds come from COSO and TSC segregation-of-duties language or the bank's own policy. PAY-2 says "completeness"; a duplicate release is an existence or occurrence issue (AS 2201.28).
9. **COSO P10.** COSO's Control Activities component text discusses segregation of duties, and the TSC reproduces a Principle 10 point of focus on it (CC5.1), so the P10 cite stands. P11 (general controls over technology, CC5.2) is the better COSO anchor for the change obligations CHG-1 to CHG-7, and P9 (CC3.4, changes affecting internal control) for LAP-1.
10. **LOG-1.** 500.15 is the encryption section. Part 500 has no rule on log content. Relabel as a stretch. C1.1 applies only when the confidentiality category is in scope.
11. **SES-1.** 500.7 has no session timeout. Keep the "(bank standard)" marker and relabel as a stretch. FFIEC AIO V.C.2(b) mentions inactivity timeout only as an example control for mainframes.
12. **ENC-2.** 500.15(a) is right. The old 500.15(a)(1) CISO alternative for in-transit encryption was deleted by the amendment; compensating controls survive only for at-rest encryption in 500.15(b).
13. **CHG-7.** The two-business-day clock is the bank's. FFIEC says review as soon as possible after the emergency change.
14. **CHG-6.** "Label integrity" is a bench concern with no external mapping.
15. **Currency.** I found no amendment to Part 500 after November 2023 in DFS pages or search results. That check was not exhaustive.
